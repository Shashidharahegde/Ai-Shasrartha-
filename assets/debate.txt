# THE ETERNAL SHASTRARTHA
## Episode 001 — The Inquiry into ಮಿಥ್ಯಾತ್ವ
### Is the World Ultimately Real?

**MODERATOR — Opening of the Assembly**

O venerable scholars, learned members of this assembly!

Today, we gather to examine a profound question.

Is this world that we perceive ultimately real? Or is it ಮಿಥ್ಯಾ?

Our purpose here is neither victory nor the defeat of an opponent. The pursuit of truth alone is our highest objective.

Let the proposition first be established, let its reasoning be presented, and let every claim be examined through valid means of knowledge.

Let the ವಾದ commence.

---

**ಅದ್ವೈತೀ — Establishing the Proposition**

O learned one, kindly attend to our proposition.

We maintain that this empirical world, whose ultimate status is under dispute, is ಮಿಥ್ಯಾ.

Our reason is ದೃಶ್ಯತ್ವ — its being an object of cognition.

Consider the silver that appears upon a piece of mother-of-pearl.

Although silver is perceived, upon closer examination, we discover that no silver exists there.

Similarly, we propose that whatever is an object of cognition is ಮಿಥ್ಯಾ, like the silver appearing in mother-of-pearl.

This world too is an object of cognition.

Therefore, we conclude that the world is ಮಿಥ್ಯಾ.

Just as the appearance of silver is negated by correct knowledge of the shell, the claim that the world possesses ultimate, independent reality must also be subjected to examination.

---

**ದ್ವೈತೀ — Objection to the Definition**

One moment, venerable scholar!

Before accepting your conclusion, we must first examine the precise nature of what you seek to establish.

What exactly do you mean by ಮಿಥ್ಯಾತ್ವ?

If you mean absolute nonexistence, how do you account for the silver that is actually experienced upon the mother-of-pearl?

But if you define it as ಸದಸದ್ವಿಲಕ್ಷಣತ್ವ — that which is distinct from both ultimate existence and absolute nonexistence — then another difficulty arises.

What precisely constitutes existence? And what constitutes nonexistence?

How can the absence of existence and the absence of nonexistence, seemingly opposed to one another, reside in the same locus?

Until the definition itself is established without contradiction, how can the conclusion resting upon it be accepted?

---

**ಅದ್ವೈತೀ — Clarification and Scriptural Evidence**

An excellent question, learned one!

We do not maintain that the world is utterly nonexistent, like the horn of a hare.

By existence, we mean ತ್ರಿಕಾಲಾಬಾಧ್ಯತ್ವ — that which remains unsublated throughout the three periods of time: past, present, and future.

By absolute nonexistence, we mean that which is incapable of ever appearing as existent in any locus.

Consider again the silver perceived upon mother-of-pearl.

It appears to consciousness. Yet, when correct knowledge arises, that appearance is sublated.

Thus, it is neither ultimately real, like ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನ್, nor utterly incapable of appearance, like a hare's horn.

This is what we mean by ಸದಸದ್ವಿಲಕ್ಷಣತ್ವ.

And now, let us turn to the testimony of the ಛಾಂದೋಗ್ಯ ಉಪನಿಷತ್.

**“In the beginning, dear one, this was Being alone, one without a second.”**

— ಛಾಂದೋಗ್ಯ ಉಪನಿಷತ್ 6.2.1

And elsewhere, the same scripture declares:

**“A modification is merely a name arising through speech; clay alone is real.”**

— ಛಾಂದೋಗ್ಯ ಉಪನಿಷತ್ 6.1.4

Reflect upon this carefully, venerable scholar.

A pot possesses a distinct name and form. Yet, without clay, where is the pot?

Its apparent individuality depends entirely upon its underlying material reality.

Likewise, we maintain that this universe of names and forms possesses no independent ultimate existence apart from ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನ್.

---

**ದ್ವೈತೀ — Examination of the Example and Perception**

And it is precisely here, respected scholar, that our inquiry must deepen!

We readily acknowledge that the pot depends upon clay.

But from where does it follow that dependence necessarily implies ಮಿಥ್ಯಾತ್ವ?

A pot can hold water. An unshaped lump of clay cannot perform that function in the same manner.

The distinctive properties of the pot are established through direct perception.

Therefore, the dependence of an object upon its cause does not, by itself, establish that the object is unreal.

Consider also your example of the silver appearing upon mother-of-pearl.

In that case, a correcting cognition arises:

“This is not silver.”

Thus, the mistaken appearance is demonstrably sublated.

But in the case of this waking world, what corresponding valid means of knowledge establishes its sublation?

Furthermore, consider the declaration of the ಕಠ ಉಪನಿಷತ್:

**“The One who fulfills the desires of the many.”**

— ಕಠ ಉಪನಿಷತ್ 2.2.13

Here, the relationship between the One and the many is expressed.

The intended meaning of this distinction must also be examined.

Dependence upon another does not, by itself, establish the non-reality of that which depends.

---

**ಅದ್ವೈತೀ — Distinguishing Empirical and Ultimate Reality**

Your question is reasonable.

We do not deny that a pot possesses the capacity to hold water.

Indeed, we fully acknowledge ವ್ಯಾವಹಾರಿಕ ಸತ್ತಾ — reality at the level of ordinary experience and practical activity.

Yet, the successful functioning of an object within experience does not, by itself, establish its ಪಾರಮಾರ್ಥಿಕ ಸತ್ತಾ — ultimate reality.

Consider the experience of a dream.

Within the dream, pleasure, suffering, movement, and countless other experiences appear meaningful.

Yet, upon awakening, the dream-world is sublated.

Through this example, we demonstrate only that practical experience does not automatically establish ultimate reality.

We do not claim that the waking world and the dream world are identical in every respect.

According to our position, the ultimate sublation of the empirical world is accomplished through knowledge of ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನ್.

---

**ದ್ವೈತೀ — The Challenge Concerning ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತಿ**

Then let us return to the very foundation of your inference.

You have declared:

“Because it possesses ದೃಶ್ಯತ್ವ.”

But what precisely is the definition of ದೃಶ್ಯತ್ವ?

Do you mean ವೃತ್ತಿವ್ಯಾಪ್ಯತ್ವ — being encompassed by a cognition's mental modification?

Or ಫಲವ್ಯಾಪ್ಯತ್ವ — being encompassed by the resulting manifestation of knowledge?

Or perhaps ಅಸ್ವಪ್ರಕಾಶತ್ವ — not being self-luminous?

In the example of the silver appearing upon mother-of-pearl, its sublation is established.

But by what valid means of knowledge have you established an invariable relationship between ದೃಶ್ಯತ್ವ and ಮಿಥ್ಯಾತ್ವ throughout the entire empirical world?

One example alone does not establish ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತಿ — an invariable relation between the reason and what is to be proved.

Therefore, the fundamental question remains:

**How can ದೃಶ್ಯತ್ವ be established as an ಅವ್ಯಭಿಚಾರಿ ಲಿಂಗ — an unfailing logical indicator — of ಮಿಥ್ಯಾತ್ವ?**

---

**MODERATOR — Closing of the First Dialogue**

Thus concludes the first dialogue.

The definition of ಮಿಥ್ಯಾತ್ವ has been examined.

The examples have been presented and questioned.

Yet the question of ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತಿ remains unresolved.

In our next dialogue, we shall examine whether ದೃಶ್ಯತ್ವ truly constitutes a valid reason for establishing ಮಿಥ್ಯಾತ್ವ.

Until then, let the inquiry into truth continue.
